Live activity, drawn across the Earth.

Orbit is a real-time 3D globe for hero sections and dashboards. Every event becomes a beam from its city to your headquarters, under the real sun, in five looks.

What you are looking at

Nothing on the globe is a picture. It is all computed, and most of it is tied to something real.

The sun is where it really is
Day, night and the terminator come from the clock, so the lit side of the globe matches the Earth right now.
Every beam is one event
An activity starts in a city facing you, arcs to your headquarters and lands with a ring and a burst of sparks. The ticker names it as it flies.
City lights follow your cities
The night side lights up around the city list you give it, on land only, and dims under cloud.
Cities surge
Now and then one city sends a rush of beams and the ticker calls it out, so the scene never settles into a loop.
Weather, aurora and a moon
Clouds drift and cast shadows, the atmosphere reddens at sunset, curtains of aurora ring both poles and the moon is lit by the same sun.
Five looks, one scene
Realistic, Cartoon, Neon, Hologram and Ink & Paper share the same globe. Switching cross-fades in about a second and re-dresses the interface with it.

It holds your display's refresh rate

On a 120 Hz screen Orbit aims for 120 frames a second, and on a 60 Hz screen for 60. It measures the display and the GPU, and when a frame would run long it gives up a little resolution instead of dropping the frame. When there is room again, the resolution comes back.

The globe above: of GPU time in a frame

  • Nothing expensive runs per frame. Terrain, clouds, the moon and the aurora are baked into textures once, on the GPU, when the page loads.
  • Particles never touch the CPU. About 40,000 drifting motes and every impact spark are moved by compute passes.
  • A beam costs nothing to keep alive. Its whole flight runs in the shader from the moment it was born.
  • The sky is not shaded behind the planet. Stars are 24,000 sprites, sharp at any resolution, and the backdrop skips every pixel the globe covers.
  • It degrades on purpose. Where WebGPU is missing, the page hands over to a Canvas 2D renderer and keeps your options.

Put it on your page

Orbit is plain ES modules with no build step. Three.js, loaded from a CDN, is its only dependency.

  1. Serve the folder

    Clone the repository and serve it from any static host.

    git clone https://github.com/studio51/orbit
npx serve orbit

  2. Embed the globe

    Frame the clean page. Add a look, or leave it off for Realistic.

    <iframe
  src="/orbit/webgpu/?embed&look=neon"
  title="Activity globe">
</iframe>

  3. Make it yours

    Set your headquarters, cities and activity types in shared/data.js, then tune the scene. 

    window.__ORBIT_SCENE__ = {
  look: 'ink',
  clouds: 0.4,
  aurora: false,
};
